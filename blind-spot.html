What's Actually Happening

The Reality

Where the optic nerve leaves each eye, the retina has no light receptors at all. This patch, the optic disc, sits about 15° from the center of your gaze toward your temple, and it covers roughly 5° by 7° of your visual field. That's big enough to hide a face across a room.

When the red dot lands on that patch, no signal from it reaches the brain. Yet you never see a black hole in your vision, even with one eye closed.

What the Later Steps Show

In step 2 the line has a gap exactly where your blind spot is, but most people see an unbroken line. In step 3 the stripes have a solid hole, and the hole disappears into stripes.

Step 4 is the strangest. The patch keeps switching colors, which is the kind of change your eyes normally can't ignore. Inside the blind spot you see steady stripes. Whatever you experience there is not tracking what is on the screen.

The Philosophical Point

It's tempting to say the brain fills in the blind spot, painting stripes over the hole the way a photo editor would. Dennett argues this gets it backwards. Filling in would need an inner screen and someone watching it.

His alternative is that the brain simply never registers the absence. No neurons report "nothing here," so nothing seems missing. The brain concludes "more of the same" and moves on. Dennett's way of putting it: the absence of a representation is not the same as a representation of absence.

He compares this to walking into a room papered with identical portraits of Marilyn Monroe. You see "lots of Marilyns" at a glance, though your eyes have only resolved a few. The rest is a conclusion your brain has drawn, not a detailed picture it has built. Neuroscientists still debate how much active filling in the visual cortex does. Either way, your experience of a complete visual field is more sparse than it feels.

Tips

  • With the right eye open, cover your left eye and look at the cross on the left
  • Start about an arm's length from the screen and move slowly closer
  • If moving your head is awkward, stay still and drag the Target Distance slider instead
  • Once the dot vanishes, switch steps without moving your head
  • Resist the urge to glance at the target. It reappears the moment you look at it.
